# Final evidence verdict (locked)

Neutral question: What is the effect of creatine supplementation on cognitive
performance, and in which direction?

Appraisal verdict: suggests_with_uncertainty (memory: low; reasoning /
processing speed: very low; sleep deprivation: low; older adults: low;
real-world benefit: very low).

Challenge outcome: verdict_weakened. Reasons accepted:
1. (high) Small-study / subgroup fragility: pooled lower CI bound 0.04 [R7];
   the older-adult SMD 0.88 rests on one or two ~32-person one-week trials
   [R23] while the 24-week trial in older women is null [R21] and the largest
   trial bounds the effect at or below 0.2 [R20].
2. (moderate) Unblinding and multiplicity: side effects RR 4.25 on creatine
   [R20]; single positive trials report one or two wins among many outcomes.
3. (moderate) Search incompleteness: 154/179 trial hits unretrieved; the 2024
   meta-analysis present only as its erratum; critiques of R7 and R1 absent.

Resolution: verdict label kept (suggests_with_uncertainty); memory certainty
lowered one step to **very low**; the older-adult subgroup is no longer
reported as a headline finding. Two unsupported statements in the appraisal
JSON are struck: R21 is not a post-2021 trial, and the rodent spatial-memory
sentence conflated two R28 statements. The "most consistent signal under
sleep deprivation" sentence is downgraded to "the direction is consistent
across a handful of small studies".

Sentence for the report: **The evidence suggests this, but uncertainty
remains.**
