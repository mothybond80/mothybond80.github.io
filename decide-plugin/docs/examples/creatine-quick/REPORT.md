# Does creatine supplementation affect cognitive performance, and in which direction?

**Verdict: The evidence suggests this, but uncertainty remains.** (certainty: very low for memory; very low for reasoning and speed; low for performance under sleep deprivation)
**Refers to the claim:** "creatine supplementation improves cognitive performance, most specifically memory, relative to placebo in adults"

## What the evidence shows (plain language)

Eight small randomised trials pooled in 2023 found a small average improvement
in memory tests on creatine versus placebo (standardised difference 0.29,
confidence interval 0.04 to 0.53) [R7, Prokopidis 2023]. The interval only
just excludes zero and the trials disagree with each other (I2 66%). In
healthy adults under about 30 the pooled effect is essentially nil (0.03).
The apparent benefit comes from a few one-week studies in people aged 66-76;
the only six-month trial in older women found nothing [R21, Alves 2013]. The
largest and best-designed recent trial, 123 adults for six weeks, found at
most a borderline working-memory signal and more side effects on creatine
[R20, Sandkuhler 2023]. Under acute sleep deprivation the direction is
consistently positive across a handful of small studies [R13, R24, R2]. A
2024 systematic review reading the same literature called the cognitive
results equivocal, while confirming that supplementation does raise brain
creatine [R6, McMorris 2024]. Whether any of this translates into a benefit
people notice is not shown: 64% of users taking creatine for cognition
perceived none [R26].

## Important counter-evidence

- [R21] Alves 2013, 24-week four-arm RCT, 56 older women: no cognitive change.
  Longest trial in the pack; carries more weight than the one-week positives.
- [R20] Sandkuhler 2023, pre-registered crossover RCT, n=123: no effect on
  reasoning or eight exploratory tasks; did not replicate [R25] Rae 2003.
- [R6] McMorris 2024, systematic review: "somewhat equivocal results".
- Published critiques of the memory meta-analysis (double counting) exist
  but were not retrieved in this run; see verdict.md.

## Confidence and gaps

Certainty is very low because the trials are small, heterogeneous, mostly
unblinded in effect (side-effect imbalance), analyse many outcomes without
correction, and the positive subgroup is fragile. Only 25 of 179 trial hits
were screened and no full text was read. A finding that would change this: a
pre-registered multi-site trial of 12+ weeks in several hundred adults with
one pre-specified memory outcome, in either direction.

## Practical recommendation

No user goals, budget or risk tolerance were supplied for this test run, so
no personalised recommendation is made. Stated generically: the evidence does
not support expecting a noticeable cognitive benefit in a healthy adult under
60; it does not rule out a small memory benefit in older adults or during
sleep deprivation; creatine monohydrate at 3-5 g/day has a well-characterised
safety profile in other literatures not appraised here. Decision assumptions:
none supplied. Value judgements: none applied. Medical note: anyone with
kidney disease should not start supplements without clinical advice; this
report is not an individual clinical assessment.
